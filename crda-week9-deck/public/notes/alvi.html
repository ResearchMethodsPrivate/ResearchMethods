Slide 7 · The second catch

start: “Thanks, Samim.”≈ 56 s3 clicks

One line: Much of CRDA’s gain is just averaging 10 models.

  1. Start

    Thanks, Samim. The second catch is about averaging. In the code, the plain score comes from one model. But the CRDA score is the average of ten models. Averaging helps all by itself. So we gave the plain model the same treatment: ten plain models, averaged. Anik and Samim repeated this on a Mac and on Concrete Strength.

  2. Click 1

    Each bar shows the gain. The amber part is just averaging. The blue part is CRDA’s own. On Energy Efficiency, for the neural network, none of the gain is averaging. For XGBoost, fifteen of the twenty-two is averaging.

  3. Click 2

    On Concrete Strength, for the neural network, seventeen of the twenty-four is averaging. For XGBoost, fourteen of the fifteen. Almost all of it.

  4. Click 3

Likely questions

Why 15 repeats?

It is what the paper used, so our numbers compare directly.

What is a repeat?

We run the experiment again on a different random split of the data. It shows how much the answer moves.

Is it fair to average the plain model?

Yes. The paper’s CRDA score averages 10 models, so we gave the plain model the same treatment.

So is CRDA useless?

No. It helps. But less than the paper’s way of scoring suggests.

Does it hold on a Mac?

Yes. Anik repeated it on his Mac and got the same pattern.

Don't say

  • That CRDA is useless. Say “it helps, but less than the paper’s numbers suggest”.
  • Exact digits. Round: 28, 29, 32.
  • Why Windows differs. Say “we do not know yet”.
